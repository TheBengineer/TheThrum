# The Thrum: Player's Guide

*Version 2.0*

> **Magic moves energy.** It never makes it. Energy is counted in **Motes**: 1 Mote ≈ 1d6 damage, or lifting 250 lb by 10 ft, or freezing a bucket of water, or shaping a cubic foot of stone.
>
> **Cost** = the Motes for the effect + range\* modifier. It has to fit within your **capacity**.
>
> **Roll** d20 + spellcasting modifier: 1 or less fails · 2–4 barely · 5–9 mediocre · 10–14 as intended · 15+ extra · natural 20 spectacular.
>
> **Leftover energy.** Under 10, whatever the spell didn't use spills out as side effects nearby. On 1 or less, it comes back at you.
>
> **Be clever.** Setting off stored energy (a gas pocket, a cracked ceiling, a dam) costs about 1 Mote, and the world pays the rest.

**\*****Range Modifier:** See the range table below. Touching is 1:1, farther takes more.

This guide is everything you need to play a caster. [Advanced Techniques](ADVANCED_TECHNIQUES.md) covers metals, gems, healing, mind magic and more, and you can pick those up when the story hands them to you.

## What magic is

Magic in The Thrum is a branch of physics. There is no spell list. You describe what you want to happen, work out the energy it takes, and pay for it. If you can describe it and afford it, it's legal.

The Flux is a basic property of the world, like gravity. Locals call its hum the Thrum. Seven laws cover all of it:

1. **Conservation.** Energy is never created or destroyed, only moved and changed. Every effect costs the energy of the change it makes.
2. **Source.** Every Mote comes from somewhere: the place around you, your own mana pool, a store such as gold or a gem, your body, or a patron who wants it back.
3. **Loss.** Shaping is never perfect. The casting roll decides how much of the energy does what you meant, and the rest spills out as side effects.
4. **Distance.** Energy is cheapest where you stand. Range costs extra.
5. **Sympathy.** Like moves like. Fire spells near a fire are cheaper, good luck with fire underwater.
6. **Release.** Triggering traps is cheap if they are set up correctly. Use Telekinesis to trip a lever, light a fuse with a spark, or send a mental signal to a friend, all for about 1 Mote.
7. **Saturation.** A place holds a limited amount of Flux. Drain it and you may need to retreat to recharge.

Two things follow from the laws. **Spread:** energy divides over everything it hits. **Interference:** opposing energies cancel, and the cancelled energy has  to go somewhere.

Once magic has turned Motes into fire, motion or cold, ordinary physics takes over. A lit barrel of lamp oil smoulders before it roars, and the DM judges what happens next from the setting.

## Your four numbers

Work these out once, at character creation and each level-up, and write them on your sheet.

| Number | What it means | How to work it out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spellcasting modifier** | Added to your casting roll | Your class's casting ability modifier (Intelligence for a wizard, and so on) |
| **Capacity** | The most Motes you can shape in one action | Half your level (round up) + spellcasting modifier |
| **Mana pool** | Motes stored in your soul, which you can carry into places with no Flux | 4 x your level |
| **Regeneration** | How fast your pool refills from the place around you | Your proficiency bonus in Motes, every 10 minutes |

| Caster | Capacity | Pool | Regeneration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Novice (level 1, +3) | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| Journeyman (level 5, +4) | 7 | 20 | 3 |
| Master (level 11, +5) | 11 | 44 | 4 |
| Archmage (level 20, +5) | 15 | 80 | 6 |

Half-casters (Paladin, Ranger, Artificer) use half their level for all of these. A level 5 caster's 7 Motes is about 4d6 at range, roughly half of a same-level adventurer's hit points. Bigger effects need cleverness, stored energy, or friends.

## Casting a spell

Every spell goes the same way:

1. **Say what you want to happen.** "I hurl that boulder at the ogre."
2. **Count the base Motes.** Use the benchmarks at the end of this guide: 1 Mote per d6 of damage, per 250 lb lifted 10 ft (4 kJ), and so on. The DM or the advisor helps with unusual effects.
3. **Add range** from the table below, using the base Motes.
4. **Check capacity.** The total has to fit within your capacity. If it doesn't, scale the spell down, find a cheaper way, or strain (see Advanced Techniques).
5. **Roll** d20 + your spellcasting modifier and read the result below.

**The range table.** The bigger the spell, the more it costs to throw it far. Read the row for the base Motes:

| Base Motes | Near (within 60 ft) | In sight | Out of sight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | +1 | +2 | +4 |
| 5–8 | +2 | +4 | +8 |
| 9–12 | +3 | +6 | +12 |
| 13–16 | +4 | +8 | +16 |
| 17–20 | +5 | +10 | +20 |

Touching the target costs nothing extra. Beyond sight is only possible if you know exactly where the target is, within the area effect of the spell, unless you hold a link to the target (a name, hair, blood, a possession), which makes it the same as in sight.

**The casting roll.** The roll doesn't change the cost. It says how well you pull the spell off.

| Total | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1 or less | It fails, and the energy comes back at you as damage, as the DM decides. |
| 2–4 | It barely works, in the least possible way. "I cast a fireball to light him on fire." Roll: 2. "His mustache is burning." |
| 5–9 | Mediocre. It works, but weakly: half the damage or duration, a wobbly aim, a short-lived result. |
| 10–14 | It works as intended. |
| 15 or more | It works with pep and vigor: a longer duration, a cleaner shape, a useful side effect. |
| Natural 20 | Spectacular, whatever the total. |

**Where leftover energy goes.** Energy is never wasted. On a total under 10, whatever the spell didn't use spills out as side effects nearby. A fireball rolls a 5: his shirt is on fire, and now so is the tapestry.

**Natural 1.** On top of the total, something extra goes wrong: Not only do you get hit with your own blast, but it hits you squarely in the face.

**Lasting effects.** Holding force (keeping something lifted) costs again every round. Light and warmth cost again every hour. Changed matter stays changed until the world changes it back: ice melts, but shaped stone stays shaped.

Damage dice still face the target's armor, cover and saving throws.

## Where Motes come from

- **The place around you.** Most of the time you cast from the local Flux, and the DM keeps track of how much is left. Old forests and teeming marshes are rich, cities and crypts are thin, and some places are dead. Every caster nearby shares the same supply.
- **Your mana pool.** You can cast from your pool anywhere, including dead zones. It refills at your regeneration rate, but only from a place that still has Flux. A long rest somewhere with Flux fills it completely.
- **Your body.** In a pinch you can burn your own life: each Life Force die you spend gives 1d6 Motes. Life Force dice are also what heals you, so this hurts later. You have one per level, at least 2. You can never turn hit points into Motes.
- **Stores and patrons.** Gold, gems and mercury hold Motes, and patrons lend them at interest. See Advanced Techniques.

Wherever the Motes come from, they still pass through you: they count against your capacity.

## Being clever: Release and Spread

**Release is where good players shine.** The world is full of stored energy: a gas pocket, a cracked ceiling, a dammed river, a boulder on a slope, a cocked ballista. Magic can unlock it for the cost of a trigger, usually 1 Mote plus range, and the world pays the rest. A chain of releases (a flask lights the gas, the gas drops a pillar) costs only the first trigger, as long as each link makes physical sense. Setting it off takes your action. Released energy doesn't care whose side you're on. Ask the DM what's stored around you.

**Spread divides energy.** You choose the shape: a beam, a cone, a burst etc. The energy divides among the creatures and objects in that shape that you mean to hit. Floors, air and clothing take a share. So a 8d6 burst on three goblins does 2d6 to each with the remaining 2 setting the floor on fire, and a narrow beam on one ogre gives it all 8d6.

**Physics is a tool.** Freezing a bucket costs 1 Mote. Freezing the puddle under a charging knight costs the same and may do more. Rope and pulleys lift things for free. The cheapest spell is usually the one that lets the world do the work.

## What a Mote buys

| 1 Mote (roughly 4 kJ) | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| 1d6 damage to one target | Fire, frost, force or lightning: it's all the same energy. Armor, cover and saves still apply. |
| Lift 250 lb by 10 ft | Holding it up costs 1 Mote each round. Leaping 10 ft straight up is the same. |
| Shove a door open or knock someone down | At touch. |
| Freeze or boil a bucket of water | The ice or steam lasts as long as nature allows. |
| Shape or shatter a cubic foot of stone or wood, roughly | A wall, a ramp, a hole. Finer work costs more. |
| Light a room for an hour | Or a blinding flash for one round. |

For scale, a farmer has 12 hit points so could be taken out with three or four Motes

## Example spells

For a level 5 caster with capacity 7.

| Spell | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Light a candle or campfire by touch | 1 |
| Shove a door open or knock someone down | 1 |
| Punch someone through a wooden wall. | 2 |
| Freeze the puddle under a charging knight, 60 ft away | 1 + 1 = 2 |
| Throw a fist-sized rock at someone 60 ft away (1d6) | 1 + 1 = 2 |
| Drop a cracked ceiling 60 ft away (the world pays the rest) | 1 + 1 = 2 |
| Fire bolt, 2d6 at 60 ft | 2 + 1 = 3 |
| Hurl a boulder, 3d6 at 60 ft | 3 + 1 = 4 |
| Lightning at a target you can see far off, 2d6 | 2 + 2 = 4 |
| Fireball, 5d6 burst at 60 ft | 5 + 2 = 7 |
| Fireball, 6d6 burst at 60 ft | 6 + 2 = 8: over capacity, so strain, or hold a torch (see Sympathy in Advanced Techniques) to bring it to 7 |

Your first few spells will take a minute to work out. After that, you'll know the common ones by heart, and the advisor can check anything new.
