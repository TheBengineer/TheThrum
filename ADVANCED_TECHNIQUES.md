# The Thrum: Advanced Techniques

*Version 2.0*

## How to use this book

The [Player's Guide](PLAYERS_GUIDE.md) is the whole game. Each section here is a separate module that adds one technique. Learn one when the story calls for it: you find a ruby, a friend is bleeding, you're locked in a lead cell. You never need to read this front to back.

**The full cost of a spell, in order:**

1. Base Motes for the effect.
2. Add range from the lookup, using the base Motes.
3. Subtract every discount that applies (sympathy, a gem, tuning). Discounts add together, but they can never cut the cost below half, rounded up.
4. Check capacity. Over it, you strain.
5. Roll d20 + spellcasting modifier − strain, plus a silver focus bonus, minus 2 if you're casting through or wearing iron.
6. Read the result. Under 10, the unused energy spills out as side effects.

Every modifier in this book is a whole number of Motes that you add or subtract. The only multipliers left are working against your medium and mind magic, and both are meant to hurt.

## Straining

*Learn it when you need more than your capacity.*

By default you work within capacity. When you say your character strains, giving it everything, you can push past it, up to double your capacity and never further. Straining is part of the casting roll: the further over you go, the bigger the penalty.

| How far over capacity | Penalty to the casting roll |
| --- | --- |
| Up to a quarter over | −2 |
| Up to half over | −5 |
| Up to double | −10 |

Work out your own ladder at level-up and write it on your sheet. For capacity 7 it reads: **8 Motes −2 · 9–10 Motes −5 · 11–14 Motes −10.** Strain also raises the chance of a total of 1 or less, which throws the energy back at you.

## Sympathy

*Learn it when you cast near fire, water, stone or a storm.*

Like moves like. Working with a matching medium makes a spell cheaper, because the medium does part of the shaping. Fire spells beside a fire, cold spells over water, lightning in a storm.

| Medium | Discount per action | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small | −1 Mote | A candle, a torch, a bucket of water, a lodestone |
| Medium | −3 Motes | A campfire, a stream, a rainstorm, a rock face |
| Huge | −6 Motes | A forge, a river, a thunderstorm, a mountainside |

**Working against your medium** (fire underwater, lightning in a dry desert wind) **doubles the cost.**

Sympathy gives no energy of its own. You can't slow a river to get Motes, and every Mote still comes from a source. Like all discounts, it can't cut a cost below half.

*Example:* a 6d6 fireball at 60 ft costs 8. Holding a torch takes it to 7, which a level 5 caster can cast without straining.

## Focuses and gems

*Learn it when you find silver or a gem.*

**Silver sharpens shaping.** Shaping a spell through a silver focus (a ring, a wand, a blade) adds to your casting roll.

**Gems store and tune.** A gem holds 1 Mote per 25 gp of value, so a 100 gp ruby holds 4. Unlike gold, a gem is sealed: it doesn't hum, doesn't leak in dead zones, and a stranger can't draw from it by touch. You charge it from your pool or the Flux, up to capacity each action, and drawing from it counts against capacity like any source. A charged gem held as a focus also discounts spells of its energy type, but never by more Motes than it holds.

| Grade | Gem discount | Silver roll bonus | Rarity | Typical price | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crude | −1 Mote | +1 | Common | 10 gp | Silver-plated ring, cloudy garnet |
| Fine | −2 Motes | +1 | Uncommon | 100 gp | Sterling wand, well-cut ruby |
| Superior | −3 Motes | +2 | Rare | 1,000 gp | Pure silver staff tip, flawless sapphire |
| Masterwork | −4 Motes | +2 | Very rare | 10,000 gp | Elven-drawn silver, ley-grown emerald |
| Legendary | −5 Motes | +3 | Legendary | Not for sale | Named relics |

**The tunings:**

- **Ruby:** heat and fire. The war-caster's stone.
- **Sapphire:** cold and water. Pairs with heat loans for cheap ice and fog.
- **Topaz:** lightning and force. Good for triggers and pushing things.
- **Emerald:** life. Discounts regrowing flesh, but doesn't supply life force.
- **Amethyst:** mind. Instead of a discount, it lowers mind magic's impedance one step while held. The most sought-after gem in The Thrum.
- **Diamond:** tunes nothing, but holds twice as much per gp and never cracks.

**Gems crack.** On a natural 1 while drawing from a gem or straining through it, the gem cracks and releases everything left in it at once, as a raw, untuned blast where it sits.

**Combining.** A gem set in a silver ring gives both: the gem's discount and the silver's roll bonus. Combining a material with a shape like this is exactly how the system is meant to be used.

*Example:* a 9-Mote fire spell, cast with a Superior ruby (−3) beside a forge (−6). The discounts add to −9, but the cost can't go below half of 9, so it costs 5.

## Metals

*Learn it when you touch a chain, wear armor, or meet lead.*

Metal is a pipe for the Thrum. It never creates energy, but each metal does one thing to it.

**Every metal conducts.** If you touch metal that is also touching your target, you cast at touch range along it: a spear stuck in a ghoul, a chain around a prisoner, an iron railing that runs to the door.

- **Distance.** Each full stretch of metal halves what's left of the spell; anything shorter is free. A stretch is 200 ft for silver and gold, 100 ft for ordinary worked metal (copper, bronze, steel), and 50 ft for rusty or scrap iron. Round the loss down. The lost energy heats the metal.
- **Thickness.** A wire carries 2 Motes per action. A rod, spear or sword carries 10. A beam, railing or anchor chain has no practical limit. Push more through and the excess bursts out as heat at your end, burning your hand and melting a wire.
- **Both ends are live.** Anyone touching the metal is part of the circuit. An enemy who grabs the other end of your chain can cast back at you just as cheaply.

| Metal | What it does | In play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lead** | Swallows | Magic can't pass through it; energy that hits it becomes heat. A lead-lined box is a dead zone inside, good for hiding magic items or holding a captured caster. A lead shield blocks a spell but is too hot to hold next round. |
| **Iron and steel** | Pulls | A spell passing within 5 ft of a large iron mass bends into it unless you pay +2 Motes to steer past. Casting through iron, or while wearing it, gives −2 on your casting roll. Casters avoid plate, and an iron portcullis shields the people behind it. When in doubt, the spell hits the most iron. |
| **Bismuth** | Pushes | A spell passing within 5 ft of a large bismuth mass veers away unless you pay +2 Motes to force it through. The deflected energy goes somewhere the DM chooses. Iron and bismuth together can bend a spell around a corner. Rare and brittle. |
| **Copper and bronze** | Carries | Conducts cleanly and does nothing else. Tripwires under rugs, ritual circles, casting on someone without touching them. |
| **Silver** | Focuses | Adds to your casting roll (see Focuses and gems). |
| **Gold** | Stores | Holds Motes (see Carrying Motes). |
| **Mercury** | Holds a shape | Holds a finished spell (see Carrying Motes). |

Shape matters as much as material. Closed loops hold energy in; open shapes like wire, rods and coins leak it or pass it along.

## Carrying Motes: gold, loops and mercury

*Learn it when you want energy on hand away from the Flux.*

**Gold stores fuel.** Gold holds 2 Motes per pound (50 gp). It starts empty, and you charge it from your pool or the Flux, up to capacity each action. Drawing from it counts against capacity. Loose charged gold is a liability: casters within 30 ft can hear it hum, anyone touching it can draw from it, and in a dead zone it leaks 1 Mote every 10 minutes. Carry it in a lead-lined purse, or wear it as a loop. A dragon's hoard is a battery.

**Closed loops hold it forever.** A gold loop never leaks, even in a dead zone, and only its wearer can draw from it.

- Ring, bracelet or anklet: 2 Motes.
- Circlet or torc: 5 Motes.
- Loops close together cancel each other, so ring-mail and stacked rings don't work. You can wear five working loops at most: one per arm, one per ankle, and one on the head or neck. That's 13 Motes at most.

**Mercury holds a finished spell.** You pour a shaped spell into a mercury vessel over several actions, up to capacity each. When the vessel breaks, or you trigger it, the whole spell releases at once, outside your capacity and with no range cost, because it was shaped at touch. A held spell leaks 1 Mote every 10 minutes. Mercury is poison: a broken vessel calls for Constitution saves, and a carried flask can break on a critical hit or a bad fall.

| Vessel | Holds | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flask (1 lb, 25 gp) | 10 Motes | Thrown as a ranged attack, 20/60 ft. On a miss it bursts a few feet off target. |
| Jug (5 lb) | 30 Motes | Two hands, thrown 10/30 ft. |
| Basin | 100 Motes | Can't be moved. A circle of casters fills it over an hour, and a heavy copper bar carries the release (a wire would melt). It can regrow a whole body, though making it alive still takes Life Force dice. |

Every store, from your mana pool to gold, gems and loops, releases through your capacity. Only mercury releases outside it.

## Heat, cold, matter and other forms of energy

*Learn it when you want ice, fog, or to reshape the world.*

**Heat and cold are ordinary energy.** 1 Mote is 1d6 of fire or frost, or warms a room for an hour. Heat moved into or out of a living body is always damage at that rate.

**Freezing and boiling water is a loan.** Turning water to ice or steam costs 1 Mote per bucket (round a cup up to 1). The rest is borrowed from the Thrum and repaid on its own when the ice melts or the steam condenses. A loan buys the change, never Motes: melting magical ice can't be captured for mana.

**Magical steam is a hot mist.** It doesn't expand like real steam, so it builds no pressure: it can scald, fog a room and fill a space, but it can't drive a piston. It hangs where it was made. Air heated by magic is ordinary hot air, and it rises.

**Shaping matter costs more for detail.** A cubic foot of stone or wood costs 1 Mote for a rough shape (a wall, a ramp, a hole), 2 for fine work (a hinge, a blade's edge), and 4 for intricate work (a lock, a gear train, jewelry). Cutting away a load-bearing piece is a Release trigger: the world pays for the collapse. Magic shapes but doesn't give skill, so anything meant to be good still needs a crafting check.

**Moving physical energy strains you.** You can take energy from one thing and put it into another, like slowing a falling rock to lift another, or pulling warmth from a room into a kettle. The Motes come from the moving thing, not your pool, but every one passes through you: it counts against capacity. A rope and pulley does the same job for free.

**It only runs one way.** Motes come from the Flux. No physical energy, whether a fire, a river or a lightning storm, ever turns back into Motes.

**Lightning** is 1d6 per Mote. **Nuclear** energy is possible in theory but takes more concentration than any caster has. **Alchemy**, pulling chemical energy out of one substance to build another (burning oil to ash to turn wood into something like plastic), is archmage work: years of study, full concentration, and a few ounces an hour at best. Starting characters can't do it.

## Counterspells

*Learn it when someone casts at you.*

Opposed energies cancel Mote for Mote. To counter a spell, you meet it somewhere along its path with opposed energy. You pick the spot and pay range to reach it. Whatever you don't cancel carries on.

The cancelled energy doesn't vanish: it bursts out where the spells meet as thunder, **1d6 per cancelled Mote, spread over everything within 10 ft.** That's base damage, so saves and cover apply. Pick your spot with care.

*Example:* a 6d6 fireball comes down a corridor. You meet it 40 ft away with 4 opposed Motes (4 + 1 range = 5). Four Motes cancel and burst as 4d6 thunder over everything within 10 ft of that spot, and the remaining 2d6 of fire carries on.

## Life and healing

*Learn it when someone gets hurt.*

Living flesh carries **life force**; dead matter doesn't. Magic can regrow an arm easily. Making it *alive* is the hard part.

**Life Force dice** (d6). You have one per level, at least 2. A long rest restores half of them (round up), or all of them in a Rich place. A hearty meal of fresh food restores 1, once a day.

**Healing has two jobs: regrow and enliven.**

- Spend a Life Force die and roll 1d6 + Con. On its own, half of it goes to regrowing flesh and half to making it alive, so it heals half the roll (round up).
- A healer's magic can do the regrowing instead, at 1d6 HP per Mote. Then each Life Force die goes entirely to making that flesh alive. A healer and patient working together heal about twice as fast.

**Grey flesh.** Flesh that's regrown but not alive is grey. It counts as temporary hit points, it can't take you above your maximum, and it can't add body parts. It feels numb and clumsy. Grey flesh that isn't made alive within about a day may turn necrotic, and the DM will tell you what that costs.

**Where life force comes from:**

- **Yourself:** your own dice, at full value.
- **A willing donor:** die for die, from their dice.
- **An animal:** only 1 in 3 gets through, and you take on some of the animal's traits for a few days. Monkey life leaves you craving bananas; wolf life makes you restless at the full moon.
- **A captive, by force:** only 1 in 4 gets through. The captive gains a level of exhaustion per die taken, and your soul is stained: nightmares and −1 Wisdom until you atone.

**Training.** A healer who practices for a month adds one Life Force die, up to their proficiency bonus in extra dice.

**The dead** have no life force left. Bringing someone back takes life force from outside and a portal reopened by ritual, and the clock is running. The DM has the details.

## Mind magic

*Learn it when you want to read, calm or command someone.*

A creature's brain and its **Chokras** are portals into the spirit realm, where its soul lives. Mind magic has to reach through the portal, and that is expensive.

**Impedance.** How much of your magic gets through depends on how well it fits the portal:

| Your magic | Cost | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Untuned | x4 | Anyone can try. The wasted energy reflects into the target's body as headaches, nosebleeds and ringing ears, which they'll notice. |
| Tuned | x2 | Casters tuned to mind (see Classes, tuning and Chokras), or holding an amethyst |
| Resonant | x1 | Your own mind, a willing target, or a strong link to that soul (a true name, a lifelong bond) |

**The soul's skin.** The target makes a Wisdom save. On a success, the energy spreads across their soul and fades harmlessly.

**What it buys** (before impedance), for about a round:

- **1 Mote:** nudge an emotion, or read surface mood.
- **3 Motes:** send a thought, or plant a short suggestion.
- **Motes equal to the target's level:** sleep or calm.
- **Twice the target's level:** a charm.

Longer effects, outright commands and repeated attempts cost more, and the DM will tell you how much.

*Example:* putting a level 3 guard to sleep by touch costs 3 Motes. Untuned that's 12, which means straining or a team. Tuned it's 6. Either way the guard gets a Wisdom save.

## Classes, tuning and Chokras

*Learn it at level-up.*

**Tuning.** A caster can be tuned to a kind of magic: fire, cold, lightning and force, life, mind, or another the DM allows. There are three equal ways in: being born with it (a class or bloodline), training (a month under a tuned teacher), or self-improvement (taking it when you open a Chokra).

- Tuned to **mind or life**, the portal impedance drops one step.
- Tuned to **anything else**, spells of that kind cost **your proficiency bonus less**, as a discount like any other.

**Opening Chokras.** Training opens Chokras, the soul's ends of the body's portals. You can open one at levels 1, 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20, after a month of training or a meaningful event in play (a ley-line vigil, a near-death experience, a mentor's rite). Each gives one permanent upgrade of your choice:

- **Wider channel:** +1 capacity. At most twice.
- **Deeper well:** your pool grows by your level. That's +1 Mote of pool per level, and it keeps growing: one Deeper well turns 4 x level into 5 x level, so a level 6 caster goes from 24 to 30.
- **Faster draw:** +1 Mote per 10 minutes to your regeneration. It speeds how fast you pull, not how much the place holds.
- **Tuning:** to one kind of magic, as above.

**Class flavor.** Each class keeps its character through one tweak. For example:

- **Sorcerers** roll 2d6 Motes per Life Force die instead of 1d6.
- **Druids** get double the sympathy discount from natural mediums.
- **Warlocks** owe no interest on their first few borrowed Motes each day.

Half-casters (Paladin, Ranger, Artificer) use half their level for capacity, pool and the rest.

## Patrons and rituals

*Learn it when you need more than you can hold.*

**Patrons** lend Motes from outside: a spirit, a god, an old thing in the hills. You repay 1.5 times what you borrowed, drained from your Life Force dice at your next long rest. Each die drained pays off 10 Motes, far more than you could get from it yourself, because a patron takes life whole. Borrow 20 and you owe 30, which costs 3 dice: a big spell tonight, and almost no healing tomorrow. Whatever you can't repay becomes a favor owed, and patrons always collect.

**Rituals** get around capacity with time. One caster or a circle pours up to capacity into a working each action until the full cost is paid. This is how great works are done: raising a wall, filling a mercury basin, bringing back the dead.

**Teamwork** is the simplest ritual. Two casters with capacity 7 can't cast a 12-Mote spell alone, but they can pour into a mercury flask together and release it at once.
