# The Thrum: DM Reference

*Version 2.0*

## Running the system

This is the DM's book. Players don't need to read it. The [Player's Guide](PLAYERS_GUIDE.md) and [Advanced Techniques](ADVANCED_TECHNIQUES.md) are written for them.

**Design goals.**

- **A few simple laws, no spell list.** Players learn the laws and invent their own spells.
- **Emergent, not enumerated.** Clever setups (stored energy, sympathy, the environment) should beat raw power, the way they do in real physics.
- **Costly enough to matter.** Big magic needs a big energy source, and finding one is an adventure.
- **Keep the table math small.** Every modifier is a whole number of Motes. If a ruling needs a calculator, look for a simpler one.

The world is generic fantasy with real physics and magic on top, in the spirit of Discworld. Characters use the standard 5e ability scores.

**What the DM tracks so players don't have to:**

- How much Flux the place has left, and where one place ends and the next begins.
- What energy is stored nearby, waiting for a trigger.
- Side effects of casting rolls under 10, and where a natural 1 sends its energy.
- Patron debts and favors owed.
- The precedent log (see The LLM advisor).

**Magic converts, then physics takes over.** The laws only cover turning Motes into physical energy. After that, real physics runs the rest, and you make an educated guess from the setting. Light a barrel of lamp oil and you get lukewarm oil burning weakly on its surface until it heats up. An alchemical solvent can flash all at once, and that's a real problem.

## Places and Flux

| Place | Flux pool (Motes) | Refill per hour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dead | 0 | 0 |
| Thin (crypts, cities) | 15 | 5 |
| Ordinary | 40 | 10 |
| Rich (old forests, teeming marshes and reefs) | 100 | 30 |
| Ley line | 250+ | 60 |

**Flux follows life, not raw physical energy.** A thundering waterfall in a barren gorge is ordinary; a quiet old forest is rich.

- Every caster within 100 ft draws from the same pool. Where one place ends and the next begins is your call, made on the fly.
- When a place is drained, it goes **dead**: plants wilt, flames gutter, and casters must pay from their pools, stores or bodies.
- Regeneration of mana pools draws from the place too. It stops in a dead zone, and casters compete for it in a thin one. Once a pool runs dry, everyone in it shares only the hourly refill.
- A long rest in a place with Flux refills a caster's pool completely.

## Stored energy for Release

What common stores give back when a trigger (usually 1 Mote plus range) sets them off. Falling and swinging things use real physics at 4 kJ per Mote. Burst is what happens in the first round, and Spread divides it over everything in the area.

| Store | damage (d6) | Area | Afterwards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Portcullis or 500 lb weight dropping 10 ft | 2 | One target under it | Blocks the way |
| Catapult or ballista, cocked | 4 | One target along its line | Needs rewinding |
| A ton of rock dropping 10 ft | 6 | 10 ft | Rubble, difficult terrain |
| Felled tree | 10 | A 5 ft line its height long | Blocks the path |
| Cracked vault ceiling | 10 to 20 | 20 ft | Buries what's under it |
| Boulder rolling down a slope | 15 to 20 | A 5 ft line down the slope | Keeps rolling |
| Flask or barrel of lamp or olive oil | 0 | Its surface | Smoulders; with a wick or strong heat it builds to a hot fire over minutes |
| Alchemical solvent (spirits, naphtha) | 10 per gallon | 10 ft per gallon | Flashes at once, burns out fast |
| Marsh gas pocket | 16 | 20 ft | Flames on the water for a round |
| Keg of black powder | 40 | 30 ft | Smoke, deafening |
| A blazing forge or kiln | 10 | 10 ft | Heat burst; the forge goes cold |
| Mill dam or millpond | 30 per round for a minute | A flood down the channel | Mud and wreckage |
| Lightning drawn from a storm | 40 | A strike point and 10 ft around | Needs a storm overhead |
| Rockslide or avalanche | 100 or more | A whole slope | Changes the map |

Chains of releases cost only the first trigger, as long as every link is physically plausible. You judge plausibility.

## Power scale

Keep the world flat. Farmers are strong from daily work, and soldiers are near peak human. Adventurers start as advanced farmers and grow past the best soldiers using magic.

| Who | HP | Life Force dice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Child or frail elder | 4 | 1 |
| Commoner | 12 | 2 |
| Trained soldier | 20 | 3 |
| Elite soldier, the best a body can get | 30 | 4 |
| Level 1 adventurer | 14 | 2 |
| Level 5 adventurer | 30 | 5 |
| Level 10 adventurer | 30 body + 20 tuned = 50 | 10 |
| Level 20 hero | 30 body + 50 tuned = 80 | 20 |

**The body caps at about 30 HP.** Training takes anyone from farmer to elite soldier, and no further. Adventurers pass the cap by tuning their own life force, so everything above 30 is magic: it can be suppressed in dead zones, drained or stolen.

Since 1 Mote is about 3.5 damage, a farmer dies to three or four Motes, and even a legendary hero falls to a couple of dozen.

## Mind magic in depth

**The model.** A creature's brain, body and Chokras are 3D portals into the 4D spirit realm. A soul is like a 4D amoeba: a skin separates the self from the surrounding Chaos, and floating inside are the far end of the brain portal and the Chokras. Mind magic has to reach through the portal and act on the soul's side. Portals have high impedance to general magic unless the caster is tuned.

**Scaling mental effects.** The costs in Advanced Techniques last one round. Apply these on top:

- **Duration:** each step up (a minute, an hour, a day) doubles the cost.
- **Control:** an effect that gives orders rather than nudging a feeling costs +50%.
- **Repeats:** every attempt on the same creature within a day, whether it worked or not, gives them +2 on their next save against mind magic. The bonus stacks.
- **Awareness:** the target feels something reaching into their mind unless the caster's casting roll beats their passive Wisdom (Insight).
- **True names:** knowing someone's true name makes the magic resonant (x1), but they get advantage on the save, because a soul guards its name.

Mind magic is still an open area. Treat these as starting values and log your rulings.

**What lives in the Chaos.** Whatever common sense says lurks in the void, from hungry mindless things to hungry thinking ones like demons. They are drawn to open portals and empty bodies. Keep it vague; the details are yours.

## The darker side of healing

**Grey flesh in detail.** Players know grey flesh is temporary HP that may go bad. The full rules:

- Sensation is deadened, as if the limb were asleep. Dexterity drops by 1 per grey limb.
- Someone who loses both legs and regrows them grey is back to full HP, but about 40% of it is temporary.
- Grey flesh that gets no life force within a day calls for a DC 10 Constitution save for each grey limb, at disadvantage if the patient didn't rest. On a failure, that limb turns necrotic until it's healed with life force.
- A limb with necrotic wounds must be amputated within a week, or the body takes poison damage equal to a third of its HP each day. Past that point the limb loses all function: it can't grip, carry or bear weight.

**Undead.** A zombie is a body regrown entirely without life force, with its portals to the spirit realm cut off. It moves, but nobody is home.

**Body as fuel.** Spending Life Force dice for Motes is legal, but HP can never become Motes. That closes the loop of turning HP into Motes and back into grey flesh.

## Death and resurrection

When the body dies, its portals close, but the soul still exists. Bringing someone back takes four steps.

1. **Rebuild the body.** Regrowing flesh works as usual (magic at 1d6 per Mote, or a mercury basin for a whole body).
2. **Find life force from outside.** A dead body has none left. It has to come from a donor's Life Force dice, a patron, or one of the very few special places with life force to spare (an ancient grove, a sacred spring). An ordinary Rich place doesn't hold enough to matter. Rebuild the body without it and you've made a zombie.
3. **Reopen the portal.** This takes tuned magic: 5 Motes per level of the dead. That's far more than one action allows, so it's a ritual: one caster or a circle pours in up to capacity each action until the cost is paid.
4. **Hold the line.** While the body's ports stand open and empty, other things come knocking: wandering souls, or something from the Chaos. Someone has to keep the portal tuned to the right soul the whole time. A strong link (hair, a true name, a loved one present) makes it easier; a weak one invites possession.

**The clock.** A soul cut off from its body erodes into the Chaos over about 10 days, and it ages fast: **10 years per day dead.** Someone revived after 3 days comes back as a soul 30 years older in their young body. They think, move and tire like an older person. Some of that fades over a few weeks, but not all. By day 10 the soul is a century older and nearly gone, so late resurrections are both harder and costlier for the person who returns.

*Example:* resurrecting a level 5 companion costs 25 tuned Motes as a ritual, plus outside life force to make the body alive.

## Rulings guide and economy

**Rules of thumb for rulings.**

- **Follow the energy.** Where do the Motes come from, and where do they end up? If energy appears from nothing or vanishes, the ruling is wrong.
- **Metal:** find the metal, then ask what it does: carry, pull, push, swallow, focus, store or hold a shape. Iron's pull picks the largest nearby mass.
- **Absorbed energy becomes heat.** A lead shield hit by 6 Motes is too hot to hold next round.
- **Shape matters as much as material.** Closed loops hold energy in; open shapes leak it or pass it along.
- **Prefer the simplest rule consistent with the laws**, and log it so the trick works the same way next time.

**Economy.**

- Charged gold sells for barely more than plain gold, since any caster can charge it. It only fetches a real premium where there are no casters around.
- High-grade focuses and gems are rare finds, not shop stock. Gem prices follow the grade table in Advanced Techniques.
- Skilled craftsmen stay in business: magic shapes but doesn't give skill.

**Still open.** Mind magic in general, which creatures can donate life force, stockpiling donors, a brake on routine resurrection, the exact penalties of soul aging, mind shortcuts (hair, blood, names), and which other 5e mechanics stay.

## The LLM advisor

The DM stays in charge. The LLM is a physics engine that the DM and players can both consult. The DM types the situation and what the player is trying to do, and the LLM answers what the laws say happens. Players can test ideas in a separate session of their own, but only the DM's session sets rulings.

**What the DM gives it:** the caster's level, modifier, capacity and mana pool; the place's Flux; what's nearby (water, fire, gas, metal, height, crowds); and the player's intent in plain words.

**What it gives back, every time, in this order:**

1. **Energy path.** Where the Motes come from and where they end up, including waste.
2. **Cost.** One line of math: base Motes, plus range, minus discounts.
3. **Can they do it?** Against capacity (and the strain penalty if over), and the Flux. If not, the closest version that works.
4. **What happens.** The outcome, including side effects the laws imply but the player didn't ask for.
5. **Ledger update.** Flux left, pool spent, debt owed.

**Precedent log.** Every ruling gets a one-line entry ("Igniting a gas pocket: 1-Mote trigger, releases about 16 Motes across 20 ft"). Paste the log back in each session so the same trick always works the same way. Answers from a player's side session go in the log only after the DM confirms them.

**Starter prompt.** Paste this, followed by all three rulebooks and the precedent log:

```
You advise the Dungeon Master of a D&D 5e campaign set in The Thrum, where magic obeys seven laws: Conservation, Source, Loss, Distance, Sympathy, Release and Saturation. The Player's Guide, Advanced Techniques and DM Reference follow this prompt; treat them as the rules. You are a physics engine, not a storyteller.

Costs are whole Motes (1 Mote is about 4 kJ, or 1d6). A spell's cost is its base Motes, plus a range surcharge based on the range step (1 per 4 base Motes, rounded up: touch +0, near + the range step, in sight + twice it, out of sight + four times it, and only with an exact location or a link), minus any discounts, which add together but never cut a cost below half. On a casting roll under 10, energy the spell didn't use spills out as side effects; on 1 or less it comes back at the caster. Casters work within capacity unless the player says they strain; straining applies a penalty to the casting roll (d20 + spellcasting modifier) of -2 up to a quarter over capacity, -5 up to half over, -10 up to double, and never past double.

When the DM describes a situation and a player's intent, answer in five parts: Energy path, Cost (one line of math), Can they do it, What happens (include side effects the laws imply), Ledger update. Never let energy appear from nothing or vanish. Magic converts energy, and then ordinary physics takes over. Reward clever use of stored energy, sympathy and the environment. Follow the precedent log exactly; if a new ruling is needed, add a one-line precedent at the end. If the rules don't cover something, say so and propose the simplest rule consistent with them.
```
